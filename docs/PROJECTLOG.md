### ADR-044 — BELCORT's high-stakes threshold raised to RM100,000 (owner ruling, 2026-07-27)
**Decision:** `clara.firms.high_stakes_amount_cents` for **BELCORT only** moves 1,000,000 → 10,000,000 (RM10,000 → RM100,000), applied live via `packages/db/deploy/wave-b-highstakes-rm100k-amendment.sql` (PR #109). The owner's first instinct — **abolish** the rule — was examined and redirected: setting the column NULL poisons `is_high_stakes` (`sum >= NULL` is NULL) across its ~20 consumers, which branch on both the true and false sides; and the categorical flags (opening / year-end / tax-affecting) were never the complaint. The two slice-era RLS fixtures (Alara Advisory, Borneo Books) keep the RM10,000 default — the amendment's own overshoot assertion proves it — and Gate F later confirmed a **new** firm still births at the default, so the ruling is genuinely per-firm. Operational effect: routine monthly vendors (RM13,000–41,040) become autopost-eligible once their sighting floors are met; six-figure bills stay human. **Follow-up owed:** no governed verb exists for this column; migration 0022 adds `set_firm_high_stakes_threshold` so it is never a raw UPDATE again.
**Why:** At RM10,000 every recurring vendor in the pilot books was permanently barred from bounded autopost, making the Wave-A2 standing-rules machinery unreachable in practice. Raising the ceiling preserves the layered control (per-rule admin-signed caps must still fit under it; `is_high_stakes` still refuses at execute) while letting the autonomy lane exist. The first amendment draft assumed single-tenant and was **refused by its own precondition** ("expected exactly 1 firm, found 3") — the assertion catching a wrong premise before the write is the pattern working.

### ADR-047 — The extraction-slice contract RATIFIED v1.0 (owner grilling, 2026-07-27)
