## 1 · Scope, and how Q9's "T1 · T2 · T3" reads

Q9 phases P4 as: *"**P4** firm tiers (T1 invite/RBAC · T2 operator-approved creation ·
T3 screens flag-hidden; admin/metering; pricing SHAPE only)"*.

**T1/T2/T3 are BUILD TRANCHES, not product tier names.** "T2 operator-approved creation" and
"T1 invite/RBAC" are not names a customer-facing tier could carry, and "T3 screens
flag-hidden" only parses as *the tier screens ship hidden behind a flag* — exactly what
"pricing SHAPE only" plus the fenced pricing sitting requires. Recorded as OWNER QUESTION 1
for a one-line confirmation; nothing downstream changes if the answer is a re-labelling. So
the **build order is T1 → T2 → T3**, while the **user journey** runs signup → approval → firm
exists → invites/RBAC. Those are different axes, not a conflict.

**In scope.** The self-serve signup door and its screens · the operator approval queue and
console · the no-firm holding state (§3) · in-firm invite issue/accept/revoke · the member
roster and role management · the tier-selection surface and metering rollup, both
flag-hidden · the R2 cream-ground entry surfaces and the R3 focus unification · the gate and
battery extensions covering all of it. Out of scope: §9.

## 3 · The structural finding: the identity gap

**This is the finding that reshapes P4, and it is not what the backend-gap ledger expected.**
The handoff records the P2 invite door as built. It is built — on the Supabase Auth side only.
On the Clara side it does nothing, and the two sides are joined by a convention with no
mechanism behind it. `clara.users.id` is documented at `packages/db/migrations/0002_foundation.sql:187` as equal
to `auth.users.id`, deliberately **without a cross-schema foreign key** ("portability"). No
trigger, no function and no door ever writes that row for a real person. Every
`insert into clara.users` in the tree is either the structural agent-identity seed, a
migration probe that rolls back, or a raw INSERT in `packages/db/seeds/0002_core_seed.sql`
running as the deploy role.

The consequence, traced end to end through `apps/web/components/invite-accept-form.tsx`:
a person accepts an invite, sets a password, gets a valid Supabase session, and lands on `/`
with **no `clara.users` row and no membership**. `clara.jwt_firm()` returns NULL, so every
RLS-scoped read returns zero rows and every governed write raises `CLR04 'actor has no
active membership'` at `clara._human_ctx`. The shell renders, and nothing in it works.

**Provenance, stated honestly: ops already knew, and the rig cannot see it.**
`docs/ops/gate-f-provisioning.md` — the Gate-F runbook, closed 2026-07-27 and retained as the
template for future firm onboarding — says it in its own words at lines 40-42: *"`clara.users.id`
**equals** `auth.users.id` by convention (0002:187) and there is **no trigger or sync** — the
row must be inserted."* So this lane did not discover the gap; it **corroborated** it, and what
is new is that a self-serve product flow cannot ride a manual owner ceremony. The reason no
test ever caught it is equally concrete: the seeds drive `create_firm` through
`set_config('request.jwt.claims', …)` (`packages/db/seeds/0002_core_seed.sql:50-51`), so the
rig has **no `auth.users` side at all** — the two-id coupling is never exercised, in either
direction.

**Three things follow, and they set P4's shape:**

- **The identity door (ask 1) is upstream of everything else in P4.** Signup, invite-accept
  and `add_member` all dead-end without it. It is built first, not last.
- **`add_member(p_user uuid, …)` is effectively uncallable from the UI for a new person** —
  not because of its floor, but because nothing can discover the uuid: `clara.users`' policy
  exposes self plus *active* firm-mates, and a not-yet-member is neither. Hence P4's invite is
  **email-keyed** (ask 3), resolving to a uuid only at accept time from `jwt_sub()`.
- **P4 must design the no-firm state explicitly** — it is a reachable, legitimate state rather
  than an accident. That is §4 E.

## 4 · The five journeys

### A · Self-serve signup — the principal registers a firm

Screens: a public `/signup` (firm name · principal name · email · password), then the holding
state (E). Two DB facts constrain the chain: the applicant has no `clara.users` row (§3), and
`create_firm` refuses any actor already holding an active membership anywhere in the estate
(`uq_membership_active_user` is global) — so "I am already staff elsewhere and want my own
firm" is refused at REQUEST time with a legible message, never discovered at approval time.

1. `supabase.auth.signUp` creates the `auth.users` row. Email confirmation is **required** —
   PRD §8's interim guardrail names email-verify as half of fail-closed admission, and it is
   the only thing standing between the queue and a bot (see owner question 11).
2. On first authenticated load the client calls the identity door (ask 1). The display name
   comes from the form; **the email comes from the JWT claim, never client input** — otherwise
   a caller could claim another person's address.
3. The client calls the registration-request door (ask 2), which refuses fail-closed on an
   existing active membership, an open request, or a flood bound (question 11).
4. The applicant lands in the holding state (E) until an operator rules.

### B · Operator approval — BELCORT rules on the queue

Screens: /admin/registrations inside the firm shell, visible **only** to a member of the firm
carrying `is_operator`. `uq_firms_one_operator` (0133:274) makes that at most ONE firm in the
estate, ever — so the console is single-tenant by construction, not a per-firm feature that
happens to be gated. Authority: **`_human_ctx(role_rank('owner'))`** and `exists(select 1 from clara.firms where
id = c.firm and is_operator)`, copying `set_wake_source_enabled` (0133:288-291) exactly. The
floor is **owner, not admin** — 0133's own comment calls owner rank "necessary but not
sufficient" and adds the operator predicate on top of it. Dropping the console to admin would
be a floor change dressed as a design detail, so it stays where the precedent puts it.

**Approval creates the firm directly — the admission token never reaches the applicant.**
This is the design's one non-obvious call. The alternative (approval mints a
`firm_admissions` token, the applicant then calls `create_firm` with it) would put a bearer
credential through the browser and add a round trip that can strand a firm half-created.
Instead `create_firm`'s core is **extracted** into a shared internal
`_create_firm_core(p_actor, p_name)` with two entrances: `create_firm` keeps its signature
and behaviour unchanged, still token-gated, still the ops escape hatch; and
`approve_firm_registration(request, op_key)` is operator-gated and passes the *applicant* as
the actor rather than the caller. That is law 81's "one deterministic core with one entrance
per surface", the same extraction PR-1c used for `_depreciation_run_due_core`.
`firm_admissions` is left byte-untouched — zero grants, zero policy, as today.

The core must preserve everything the LIVE 0017 body does, including opening the
`onboarding_plans` firm-scope plan and revision 1 and returning `plan_id` alongside
`firm_id`. A screen written against 0004's or 0005's return shape would silently drop it —
the superseded-body class; the annex pins the byte evidence. Rejection is a first-class verb
carrying a reason, never a silent deletion.

### C · In-firm invite — staff join an existing firm

Screens: an invite dialog on /admin/members; the existing public /invite/[token] accept
page, extended.
Issue (admin+): `invite_member(email, role, op_key)` (ask 3) writes a `clara.firm_invites`
row and returns the invite id. It does **not** create a user and does not send email.
Sending email needs the Supabase service-role key, which the browser must never hold, so the
mail step lives in a server-only Route Handler that (1) verifies the caller's session,
(2) calls `invite_member` **as the caller** so the DB performs the authority check, and
(3) only on a successful return uses the service key to send the Supabase invite. The UI is
never the wall; the handler is a courier, not a guard. This is judgement logic and takes the
independent review pass under review law 1.

Accept: `accept_invite(token, display_name, op_key)` (ask 4) is one transaction that calls
`_claim_identity_core`, then `_add_member_core`, then consumes the invite. **The wall is that
the JWT's verified email must equal the invite's email** — without it, anyone holding the
token binds it to their own account. That is a second, independent wall on top of Supabase's
`verifyOtp` binding, which the P2 surface already performs and which stays exactly as built.

**Why `_add_member_core` and not a fresh INSERT.** `add_member`'s live body (0005:677-705)
carries four walls a hand-written membership INSERT would silently drop: the **agent-identity
refusal** (`is_agent` → `CLR10`, its own HIGH-11 wall), the per-firm `FOR UPDATE` on
`clara.firms`, `_reserve_op` idempotency, and the already-belongs check against the global
unique index. A door minting memberships without them would be the one path in the estate
admitting the agent's deterministic uuid into a firm — and the *unauthenticated-adjacent* one,
reached with only an emailed token. The core carries all four; each entrance keeps its own
`_reserve_op`/`_finish_op` under its **own** verb string, so the audit row reads
`accept_invite`, never `add_member`, while the `member.added` event fires in the core because
the fact is the same either way (annex 1 §D.1 splits it row by row). Both cores are judgement
logic and take the independent review pass under review law 1, as does the courier above.

The existing accept page keeps its click-gate, hard-coded `type: "invite"`, subject-binding
check and `router.replace("/")`; P4 adds one step after the password is set — the
`accept_invite` call, whose refusal renders verbatim like any other.

### D · Members and roles — the RBAC surface

Screens: /admin/members — the roster, the pending-invite list, role change, remove.

Reads ride one new consolidated view (ask 5), because today name, email and status live on
three surfaces with three different visibility rules — `users_visible` (names, any status),
`clara.users` (email, active only, no rank floor), `firm_memberships` (role and status).
Consolidating them is also the natural place to put a rank floor on email, the standing
Track-A debt row. Writes are the three existing doors unchanged — `add_member`,
`set_member_role`, `remove_member`; P4 adds only `revoke_invite` (ask 6).

**The last-owner wall is not pre-empted in the UI.** `_tf_guard_last_owner` refuses `CLR09`
when the last non-agent active owner would be demoted or removed; the UI lets the click happen
and renders the refusal verbatim, because pre-disabling would be the UI guessing the DB's
answer and no control may invent a wall. Every act re-reads the roster — no optimistic update.

**Affordance shaping, as a rule:** whole surfaces below the caller's rank are **hidden** from
nav; in-context verbs above it are **shown disabled with the required rank named**. Hiding
avoids a dead end; disabling teaches the ladder. Neither is a security boundary — `_human_ctx`
is. There is no re-activation verb and P4 does not ask for one: re-inviting a removed person
creates a fresh membership row, which the partial unique index permits.

### E · The holding state — an authenticated session with no firm

A new route renders honestly for a session with no active membership: registration pending,
registration rejected with its reason, or invite-expected. It is the one screen that must
work with `jwt_firm()` NULL. **Where the check lives — and the trap in the obvious answer.** Not in the proxy: the JWT
carries no firm claim (`jwt_firm()` is a DB lookup), so it would need a data round trip, and
the proxy is deliberately the auth gate only. The obvious alternative is the firm shell's
layout — but **`(firm)` is not the only authenticated surface**. `app/(full)/` (both Clara
full-screen escalation routes) and `apps/web/app/api/runtime` are its **siblings**, not its
children: a route group adds no URL segment and wraps nothing outside itself, so a check placed
only in `(firm)/layout.tsx` leaves a no-membership session able to reach /clara/[threadId] and
the runtime proxy — landing in exactly the NULL-`jwt_firm()` state this section eliminates.

So the check is **one implementation with three entrances**, the same discipline as the cores
in §5: a single `requireFirmScope()` server helper called by `(firm)/layout.tsx`,
`(full)/layout.tsx`, and the API route handler. The layouts redirect to the holding route; the
API route **refuses with a 403**, because a redirect is not an answer to a data request. A
fourth authenticated surface later means calling the helper — the seam is visible, not
implicit. This is judgement logic and takes the independent review pass under review law 1, and
the fail-closed direction holds at every entrance: a failed read and an empty result both route
to the holding state or the 403, and both grant nothing.

**Two authenticated surfaces do NOT call the helper, and both exemptions are deliberate** —
written down because an unexplained exemption is the thing a later lane "fixes".
`apps/web/app/logout/route.ts` is exempt **by necessity**: a session with no firm must still be
able to log out, and gating logout on membership would strand exactly the people the holding
state exists for (its own same-origin and POST-only walls are the ones that matter there).
The proposed mail courier (annex 2 §E) is exempt **on principle**, and is this rule's first test
case: it calls `invite_member` as the caller, and `_human_ctx(role_rank('admin'))` already
raises `CLR04` for any caller without an active membership, so **the DB is the wall** — adding
a scope check in front would be the courier pretending to be a guard, the confusion §4 C's
ordering exists to prevent. The rule both imply: **a surface calls `requireFirmScope()` when it
renders or returns firm-scoped data on its own authority, and does not when a governed door is
already the wall.**

The proxy changes by exactly one line — `/signup` joins `PUBLIC_PATH_PREFIXES`
(`apps/web/lib/supabase/proxy.ts:42`). The holding route is **not** public: it requires a
session, it just does not require a firm.

### F · Tiers — a display, not a chooser (T3, flag-hidden)

Screens: /admin/tiers, behind the flag. **Its shape follows owner question 5, and this design says so rather than assuming the prettier one.** If tier assignment is operator-only
in beta — my own recommendation — the firm-side surface is a **read-only display**: current
tier, what it includes, this month's metered usage from `get_llm_usage_summary`, and every
amount a placeholder. A chooser would be a control that cannot act, which the house law
forbids. **A selection flow, a RadioGroup vendoring, and a Mobbin "tier selection panel"
reference all survive only if you overrule question 5** and put assignment in the firm
owner's hands. Designing the chooser first and disabling it later would be the fake control
arriving by the back door.

## 5 · BACKEND-ASKS

Eleven asks. Each is a fail-closed, receipted, `_human_ctx`-floored door or a granted read
surface riding the uniform ADR-061 ladder as its own train — none is invented client-side.
Signatures, refusal codes and receipts are in annex 1 §D. Asks 1-4 are load-bearing for any P4
screen at all; 8-11 serve the flag-hidden tier tranche and can follow.

**Three cores, not three parallel writers (law 81).** P4 mints identities and memberships
through paths that already exist, so the extraction discipline applied to `create_firm` in
§4 B applies twice more: `_claim_identity_core` (asks 1 and 4) and **`_add_member_core`
(ask 4 and the live `add_member`)**. The second matters most — see §4 C.

**Two seams a later lane could build a cap through, closed here in words.**
`firm_tiers.ordinal` is **display order and nothing else** — never an entitlement rank, never
a comparison a guard reads. And an **"included allowance" is a billing figure, not an
enforcement input**: it belongs on the invoice, computed against the metering ledger at billing
time, and is deliberately NOT a column any runtime path reads. Migration 0105 exists because a
cap was built once and had to be deleted; neither ask 9 nor ask 10 stores a number a brake
could be hung on later.

**Ask 8 carries a dependency that is a ceremony, not code.** `clara.firms.is_operator` exists
and **zero firms carry it** — 0133's own tail raises if any firm is already marked. Marking
BELCORT is an unperformed raw ops act, and until it runs the operator console is unreachable
by anyone and cannot be smoke-tested. Correct fail-closed behaviour, but it needs scheduling
alongside the ask-8 train rather than discovering during it.

## 6 · R2 and R3 — three collisions to surface

Measured with the shipped gate's own functions. **The execution is annex 2 §H**; the findings
and the decisions they force are here, each pointing at its owner question.

1. **R2 is, against today's tokens, visually a no-op** (question 2). The entry pages render on
   `--shell` today; cream vs shell measures **1.0082**, an RGB delta of (0, −1, −3) — a 3/255
   max-channel change no display resolves. Cream only reads against **white** (1.0814). So the
   founder note was granted for a change no one can see, and the real choice is provenance
   versus composition: a white card on cream, which then needs a card-edge token, because the
   card is **1.081** and `--border` **1.195** against cream. Text on cream needs no decision —
   all ten semantic pairs pass 4.5:1, tightest `muted-foreground` at **4.636**.
2. **R3 fails SC 1.4.11 on every ground, not just cream** (questions 3, 3b) — and Q7 ruled AA
   the formal bar. The composited halo reads 2.317 cream · 2.310 shell · **2.363 white** ·
   2.313 surface-subtle · 2.280 secondary · 2.245 accent, while the solid `--focus` outline the
   base rule renders today passes everywhere at 6.18–6.70. Two facts drive the fix: **nothing
   rescues the default Button** (`--ring` and `--primary` are the same hex, so its swapped
   border against its own fill is **1.000**, with the halo at 2.674 outside), and **executing
   R3 literally makes matters worse** — a plain link inherits the compliant base outline at
   6.20 today, and unifying on the ring strips it, growing the failure population from the ten
   components carrying the idiom now to *every focusable element in the app*. The binding
   ground for an alpha fix is `accent` at **66%**; 65% measures **2.970** and would red CI the
   day after approval, so the recommendation is **70%** (≥0.270 margin, annex 1 §C.1).
3. **Form-field boundaries fail the same criterion today** (question 4). `--input` reads
   **1.728** on white, 1.611 shell, 1.598 cream, 1.594 surface-subtle, and
   `apps/web/components/ui/input.tsx` ships `bg-transparent` — no fill distinguishes the field
   from the page, so the border is the *only* identifier and the bar applies squarely. Live on
   today's login and invite pages and every P3 form; P4 only makes it more visible.

**And a gate blind spot, independent of P4.** Both existing focus pairs use
`fg: (h) => h("focus")` — the solid token — and cite only the base outline rule in their
`source`. Ten components render the translucent idiom and no pair sees it, so the gate is
green on a treatment that is not there. **It also has no `--strict` flag**: one existed and was
retired at the P3 finale, and it now fails on any failing pair unconditionally, so any

## 9 · NON-GOALS

- **Pricing amounts** — fenced to the owner's sitting. Every price renders through one named
  placeholder component stating the amount is pending; never a number, and never `RM0` or an
  em-dash, both of which read as "free". **Payment wiring** likewise: Stripe is the named
  provider (Q-B), the checkout shell stays provider-agnostic, and none of it lands here.
- **Capability gating by tier** (owner question 6 — laws 76/78/81 forbid it) and **usage caps
  or brakes of any kind**, including any "included allowance" enforcement (see §5's note).
- **Client-level permissions** — roles are firm-wide; per-client access is not in the schema.
  **Multi-firm membership** likewise: the global unique index makes one active membership per
  person an estate-wide invariant and P4 designs within it (owner question 7).
- **The mobile corridor** (Q6 scope — the entry surfaces are single-column and will behave,
  but no corridor work is claimed) and **new `parts[]` wire types** (P4's surfaces are pages
  and forms; the catalog and its tsc guard are untouched, and the four-part bump stays P6).
- **Narrowing the 0002 email grant** — ask 5 creates the floored surface that makes it
  possible later; the base-table revoke needs its own consumer census (Track-A row).
  **Re-activating a removed membership** is not asked for either: re-invite instead.

## 10 · OWNER QUESTIONS

*Each carries this lane's recommendation; none is resolved here.*
1. **Do Q9's T1/T2/T3 read as build tranches** (invite/RBAC → operator-approved creation →
   flag-hidden tier screens) rather than product tier names? *Recommend: yes — the design is
   written to it; a re-label changes no work.*
2. **R2 is visually a no-op against today's tokens** (cream vs the entry pages' current shell
   ground is a 3/255 max-channel delta). Accept it as a provenance-only change, or pair the
   cream ground with a white card so the front door actually reads as one? *Recommend: the
   white card — but it needs a card-edge token decision, because the card is 1.081 and its
   border 1.195 against cream.*
   **2b — a fourth entry surface.** R2 names three (login, signup, invite-accept). The
   holding state of §4 E is a fourth, and it is the one an applicant stares at for as long as
   approval takes. *Recommend: include it — but as your explicit ruling, not as a side effect
   of the route group I put it in. If you exclude it, it moves out of `(entry)` and the
   grouping stops being the mechanism.*
3. **R3 fails SC 1.4.11 on every ground, not just cream**, and executing it literally
   **extends** the failure — stripping the compliant base outline from every plain link and
   row. Composited, the halo measures 2.245–2.363 against a 3:1 bar. *Recommend: **70% alpha**,
   not 65 — `accent` is the binding ground at 66%, and 65% measures 2.970, which would red CI
   the day after you approved it. 70% clears all six gated grounds with ≥0.270 margin.*
   **3b — the Button is a separate decision.** No halo alpha fixes it: `--ring` and
   `--primary` are the same hex, so the swapped border against a default Button's own fill is
   **1.000**. *Recommend: rule 3 and 3b separately — a Button-scoped treatment (offset ring, or
   a contrasting border token for filled variants) may be the cheaper whole answer, and
   pre-committing to the global alpha move would foreclose it.*
4. **Form-field boundaries fail SC 1.4.11 today**, 1.728 on white, and no gate row sees it.
   The fix is darkening `--input`, a token-contract recut. *Recommend: take it, in the same
   §9 recut PR R3 already owes clarabook-frontend, and add the gate rows here.*
5. **Who may change a firm's tier?** *Recommend: the operator only, for beta. A self-serve
   upgrade button with no payment wired would be a fake control, which the house law forbids.*
6. **Confirm tiers are a BILLING concept only** — price and included allowance, never a
   capability gate or a usage brake. Laws 76, 78 and 81 all forbid the gating reading
   (migration 0105 exists to delete a cap that was built once), and the ruled PRD §8 shape —
   base monthly tier plus metered overage — is compatible with them. *Recommend: confirm
   billing-only, so no lane later builds a T1 feature wall.*
7. **One active membership per person, estate-wide** is a hard DB invariant: a principal who
   is staff elsewhere cannot register their own firm without being removed there first, and a
   BELCORT operator cannot hold a client-firm membership. *Recommend: keep it and refuse early
   with a legible message; changing it is a schema change with wide RLS blast radius.*
8. **Signup posture and the deploy obligation.** `apps/web/README.md` instructs that Supabase
   "Allow new users to sign up" be **off**, calling invite-only "a product invariant, not a UI
   choice". P4 inverts that. *Recommend: the flag flips in the same PR that ships the approval
   gate, never before — a security mechanism is not weakened ahead of its replacement — and
   the README is re-worded in that PR to "self-serve signup is open; admission is fail-closed
   behind the operator approval queue".*
9. **The `is_operator` ceremony.** Zero firms carry the flag; marking BELCORT is a raw audited
   ops act. *Recommend: schedule it with the ask-8 train — the console cannot be smoke-tested
   before it runs.*
10. **Metering has no rank floor** — `get_llm_usage_summary` lets a viewer read firm spend,
    while `audit_log` and agent receipts floor at bookkeeper+. *Recommend: an admin floor in
    the DB (ask 11); a UI-only fix would not be a wall.*
11. **Signup is an unauthenticated queue, so it can be flooded.** Nothing in the design stops
    one address re-applying after every rejection, or a script filling the operator's console
    with requests. Email verification raises the cost but does not bound it. *Recommend two
    bounds, both in the DB where they are walls rather than manners: a **cooloff** before a
    rejected applicant may re-apply (the rejection row already carries `decided_at`), and a
    **per-email request cap** over a rolling window. Both refuse at `request_firm_registration`
    with a legible reason. Your call on the numbers; I would start at 7 days and 3 requests.*

## 11 · Mobbin grounding — UNMET, and why

The handoff's rule is that every NEW product flow is grounded in a Mobbin reference, and the
task named the MCP as available in-session. **It is not authenticated** — the authenticate
call returns an OAuth URL needing a browser round trip this lane cannot complete
autonomously. No Mobbin references are cited here, because citing references this lane did not
consult would be exactly the fabrication the repo's evidence laws exist to prevent. The
obligation is recorded as open, not quietly dropped. Three flows need grounding before build, conventional enough that one authenticated sitting
closes them: multi-tenant **signup with an approval hold**, an **operator approval queue**,
and a **members + roles table with an invite dialog**. A fourth — a **tier selection panel** —
is only needed if owner question 5 is overruled (§4 F). R5 already approved adding the server
to `.mcp.json`; doing that in the same sitting makes this reproducible for every later lane.
