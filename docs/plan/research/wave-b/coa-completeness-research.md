# CoA completeness — official-source research (2026-07-24)

> Which ledger lines Malaysian tax and company law require to be SEPARATELY IDENTIFIABLE.
> Fetched from AGC (legislation), LHDN public rulings, MASB and SSM. Cited, not recalled.

## LHDN / Income Tax Act 1967 — lines that cannot be merged

### Verified facts

- ITA 1967 s.33(1) (AGC online updated text, as at 1 Nov 2023): adjusted income is gross income less 'all outgoings and expenses wholly and exclusively incurred during that period by that person in the production of gross income from that source'. s.39(2) declares that s.33 is NOT an express provision overriding s.39 except as to s.39(1)(a)-(d) - so the s.39 prohibitions bite even on genuine business expenses.
- ENTERTAINMENT - ITA s.39(1)(l) (verbatim, as at 1 Nov 2023) disallows 'a sum equal to fifty percent of any expenses incurred in the provision of entertainment including any sums paid to an employee of that person for the purpose of defraying expenses incurred by that employee in the provision of entertainment', with EIGHT provisos that escape the 50% cut: (i) entertainment to his employees except where incidental to entertainment for others; (ii) entertainment by a person carrying on a business that consists of/includes providing entertainment for payment to clients/customers in the ordinary course; (iii) promotional gifts at trade fairs or trade/industrial exhibitions held OUTSIDE Malaysia for promotion of exports from Malaysia; (iv) promotional samples of products of the business; (v) entertainment for cultural or sporting events open to members of the public, wholly to promote the business; (vi) promotional gifts WITHIN Malaysia consisting of articles incorporating a conspicuous advertisement or logo of the business; (vii) entertainment related wholly to sales arising from the business; (viii) a benefit/amenity to an employee consisting of a leave passage to facilitate a yearly event WITHIN Malaysia involving the employer, the employee and the employee's immediate family members.
- ENTERTAINMENT - LHDN Public Ruling No. 4/2015 (29 July 2015) para 5: entertainment wholly and exclusively incurred under s.33(1) 'is allowed a deduction of fifty percent (50%) only unless that expense falls within any of the specified categories in provisos (i) to (viii) of paragraph 39(1)(l) ... then it qualifies for a deduction of one hundred percent (100%)'. Para 3.4 defines 'entertainment related wholly to sales' (proviso vii) as entertainment directly related to sales provided to customers, dealers and distributors BUT EXCLUDING SUPPLIERS. Para 11: 'Taxpayers are required to keep records in respect of entertainment expense incurred.'
- ENTERTAINMENT - PR 4/2015 Table 1 shows the treatment turns on facts a merged account destroys: 100% - annual dinner for employees (proviso i), gift WITH business logo for a customer's annual dinner (proviso vi), free trip as a sales incentive to a sales agent for achieving a sales target (proviso vii), entertainment at the launch of a new product to potential/existing customers (proviso vii). 50% - gift WITHOUT business logo, gift of flowers for a customer's new outlet, entertainment to SUPPLIERS, festive hampers for customers (all 'not included under provisos (i) to (viii)'). NIL - entertainment to a potential customer in a closed transaction, wedding gift to a customer, entertainment to employees of RELATED companies, entertainment for the company's own AGM, cash contribution to a customer's annual dinner (all 'not wholly and exclusively incurred under subsection 33(1)').
- LEAVE PASSAGE - ITA s.39(1)(m): 'notwithstanding subparagraph (l)(i), and subject to subparagraph (l)(viii) any expenditure incurred in the provision of a benefit or amenity to an employee consisting of a leave passage within or outside Malaysia' is disallowed. So the ONLY deductible employee leave passage is the proviso (viii) local yearly-event one; every other leave passage is a permanent add-back.
- PRE-COMMENCEMENT (general rule) - LHDN PR No. 11/2013 'Pre-Operational Business Expenditure' (18 Nov 2013, replaces PR 2/2010) para 6.1: 'Generally, expenses incurred by a person prior to the commencement of his operations or business would not be allowable as a deduction against the gross income of his business as they are not wholly and exclusively incurred in the production of the income.' Para 6.2: Schedule 4B and specified Rules allow certain exceptions. Para 5.2 sets the commencement test (activities in the course of business / part of the income-producing process, as distinct from preparatory activities).
- INCORPORATION EXPENSES - PR 11/2013 para 8.1-8.2: the governing rules are the Income Tax (Deduction For Incorporation Expenses) Rules 2003 [P.U.(A) 475/2003] and the Amendment Rules 2005 [P.U.(A) 472/2005]. With effect from YA 2004, for a company incorporated in Malaysia on or after 13.9.2003 with an authorised capital of NOT MORE THAN RM2,500,000, only SIX categories are deductible: (a) cost of preparing/printing the memorandum, articles and prospectus and of circulating/advertising the prospectus; (b) cost of registering the company and the statutory documents together with fees and stamp duties payable; (c) cost of drawing up the preliminary contracts and stamp duties; (d) cost of printing debentures and stamp duty and of share certificates and letters of allotment; (e) cost of the company seal; (f) underwriting commission. (Predecessor: Income Tax (Deduction of Incorporation Expenses) Rules 1974 [P.U.(A) 134/1974], same six heads but an authorised-capital ceiling of RM250,000 - PR 2/2002 para 3.4.1, now superseded.)
- INCORPORATION EXPENSES - timing and balance-sheet treatment: PR 11/2013 para 8.3 - the incorporation expenses 'shall be deemed to have been incurred in the basis period for a year of assessment in which the company commences its business.' Para 8.4 - 'The deduction is to be made in the tax computation for the year of assessment indicated in paragraph 8.3 above IF THE EXPENSES HAVE BEEN CAPITALISED IN THE COMPANY'S BALANCE SHEET. For incorporation expenses which have been charged out in the company's profit & loss account, adjustments would be made to the tax computation if there are expenses other than those specified in paragraph 8.2.' So the deduction is driven off the ledger detail, not the P&L line.
- INCORPORATION EXPENSES - the mixed-bundle trap is explicit. PR 11/2013 Example 5: a company's claimed incorporation costs of RM4,070 comprised preparation/printing of M&A RM1,000, cost of registering the company RM2,500, COMPANY SECRETARIAL FEES RM400, SERVICE TAX RM20, TRAVELLING (reimbursement) RM100, MISCELLANEOUS (photostating etc) RM50. Ruling: 'Only the expenses on preparation and printing of memorandum and articles of association and cost of registering the company amounting to RM3,500 are eligible for deduction.' A single 'Preliminary expenses' account cannot be adjudicated.
- SCHEDULE 4B - ITA Schedule 4B [Sections 43 and 44] 'Qualifying Pre-Operational Business Expenditure' para 1: it applies ONLY to expenditure incurred by a company RESIDENT IN MALAYSIA in connection with a proposal to undertake investment in a business venture AS APPROVED BY THE MINISTER in a country OUTSIDE Malaysia. Para 2 limits it to (a) expenses directly attributable to feasibility studies; (b) expenses directly attributable to market research / obtaining marketing information; (c) fares for overseas travel by a company representative for those purposes, and actual accommodation and sustenance 'subject to a maximum of four hundred ringgit per day'. Para 3: it is deducted UNDER SUBSECTION 44(1) - i.e. against aggregate income, NOT against business gross income. Para 4: unabsorbed amounts carry forward to the first later year with a 'defined aggregate'.
- OTHER PRE-COMMENCEMENT RULES - PR 11/2013 para 3.2 lists the subsidiary legislation in play: P.U.(A) 61/1992 and 111/1995 (approved training), P.U.(A) 160/1996 (pre-commencement of business training expenses), P.U.(A) 475/2003 and 472/2005 (incorporation), P.U.(A) 135/2006, 65/2007, 361/2008, 401/2009, 76/2012. Para 10.2: pre-commencement business TRAINING expenses get a SINGLE deduction only if (a) the training imparts basic skills to enable the company to commence business, (b) incurred within ONE YEAR prior to commencement, and (c) of a kind allowable under s.33. Para 10.3: no deduction if the company receives Government training grants or is claiming double deduction under the Approved Training Rules. PR 11/2013 also covers separate regimes for REIT/PTF establishment expenditure, Islamic stock broking company establishment expenditure, employee-recruitment pre-commencement expenses, and franchise fee.
- HIRE PURCHASE (statute) - ITA Schedule 3 para 46 (verbatim): 'Where a person incurs capital expenditure under a hire purchase agreement on the provision of any machinery or plant for the purposes of a business of his, he shall for the purposes of this Schedule be taken to be the owner of that machinery or plant; and the qualifying expenditure incurred by him on that machinery or plant in the basis period for a year of assessment shall be taken to be THE CAPITAL PORTION OF ANY INSTALMENT PAYMENT (or, where there is more than one such payment, of the aggregate of those payments) MADE BY HIM UNDER THE AGREEMENT IN THAT PERIOD.'
- HIRE PURCHASE (ruling) - LHDN PR No. 5/2014 'Ownership and Use of Asset for the Purpose of Claiming Capital Allowances' (27 June 2014) para 10.1: 'The installment payments are made up of capital portion (deposit and capital repayments) and revenue portion (HP interest).' Para 10.1.1: 'HP interest is deductible against gross income in determining the adjusted business income of the hirer. Deposit and capital repayments are QE eligible for CA' - initial allowance computed on QE incurred EACH YEAR through the HP period, annual allowance on CUMULATIVE QE. Para 10.1.2: 'QE for a HP asset refers to the capital expenditure ACTUALLY INCURRED by the hirer. Therefore CA is computed based on capital installment payments that HAVE BEEN MADE for the year of assessment and NOT based on the amount listed in the schedule of the HP agreement.' On early settlement QE is the total capital repayments actually made; instalments in the schedule that were not paid are not QE. Legal title stays with the financier until final settlement, but the hirer is the deemed/beneficial owner for CA.
- MOTOR VEHICLES - ITA Schedule 3 para 2(2): for a motor vehicle other than one licensed by the appropriate authority for commercial transportation of goods or passengers, qualifying plant expenditure incurred on or after the first day of the basis period for YA 1991 'shall be limited to a maximum of fifty thousand ringgit', increased to not more than one hundred thousand ringgit where the vehicle was purchased on or after 28 October 2000, has NOT been used prior to purchase, and the total cost does not exceed one hundred and fifty thousand ringgit. Mirror restriction on the revenue side: ITA s.39(1)(k) disallows motor-vehicle RENTALS in excess of RM50,000 (RM100,000 on the same new/RM150,000-cost test), with a further proviso that the aggregate deduction across the YA and subsequent YAs must not exceed that cap 'in respect of that motor vehicle' - i.e. a per-vehicle cumulative memo is required.
- DIRECTORS' FEES - Companies Act 2016 s.230 (AGC updated text as at 1 Aug 2022), verbatim: '(1) The fees of the directors, and any benefits payable to the directors including any compensation for loss of employment of a director or former director - (a) of a public company; or (b) of a listed company and its subsidiaries, shall be approved at a general meeting. (2) In the case of a private company, the Board may, subject to the constitution approve the fees of the directors and any benefits payable to the directors... (3) Any approval made under subsection (2) shall be recorded in the minutes of the directors' meeting and the Board shall notify the shareholders of the approval of the fees within fourteen days from the date of the approval. (4) [members holding at least 10% of total voting rights who consider the payment was not fair may, within 30 days after knowledge, require a resolution] (5) Unless an approval has been obtained through a resolution passed under subsection (4), THE PAYMENT SHALL CONSTITUTE A DEBT DUE BY THE DIRECTOR TO THE COMPANY.' Fines: up to RM3,000,000 for s.230(1) breach, up to RM250,000 for s.230(3).
- DIRECTORS' FEES vs SALARY (tax) - ITA s.13(1)(a) includes in employment income 'any wages, salary, remuneration, leave pay, FEE, commission, bonus, gratuity, perquisite or allowance (whether in money or otherwise) in respect of having or exercising the employment' - so a director's fee is the director's employment income, the same class as salary. But ITA s.25(1) taxes it on RECEIPT: 'where gross income from an employment is receivable in respect of any particular period, it shall, WHEN RECEIVED in the relevant period, be treated as the gross income of the relevant person for the relevant period' (the old s.25(2), (2A), (3), (4) and (5) were all deleted by Act 773). The company deducts on the s.33(1) incurred/accrual basis (subject to s.33(4)-(5) where a sum payable is not yet due), so an accrued-but-unpaid directors' fee sits in a different year for payer and recipient.
- DIRECTORS' FEES vs SALARY (EPF) - EPF Act 1991 (Act 452, AGC reprint incorporating amendments up to 1 January 2006) s.2: 'wages' means 'all remuneration in money, DUE TO AN EMPLOYEE UNDER HIS CONTRACT OF SERVICE or apprenticeship whether agreed to be paid monthly, weekly, daily or otherwise and includes any bonus, commission or allowance payable by the employer to the employee ... but does not include - (a) service charge; (b) overtime payment; (c) gratuity; (d) retirement benefit; (e) retrenchment, lay-off or termination benefits; (f) any travelling allowance or the value of any travelling concession; or (g) any other remuneration or payment as may be exempted by the Minister.' And 'employee' means 'any person, not being a person of the descriptions specified in the First Schedule, who is employed UNDER A CONTRACT OF SERVICE or apprenticeship'. The EPF hook is therefore the contract of service, not the office of director - so fees paid for holding office (no contract of service) fall outside 'wages' while salary under a service contract is inside.
- EMPLOYER RETIREMENT CONTRIBUTIONS - ITA s.34(4): where the employer contributes to an APPROVED scheme in respect of an employee, the deduction is 'an amount equal to the contribution or NINETEEN PER CENT of the employee's remuneration as so determined for the period for which the contribution is made, WHICHEVER IS THE LESS' (with a pro-rating rule in s.34(4)(b)). ITA s.39(1)(d) disallows outright 'any amount in respect of any payment to any pension, provident, savings, widows, widowers and orphans or other similar fund or society which is NOT an approved scheme.'
- SECRETARIAL & TAX FILING FEES - the current authority is LHDN Guideline ref. LHDN.AG.600-1/7/3, 'Pindaan Garis Panduan Potongan Bagi Perbelanjaan Berhubung Dengan Yuran Kesetiausahaan dan Yuran Pemfailan Cukai Mulai Tahun Taksiran 2022', dated 10 November 2023 (s.k. LHDN.01/35/(S)/42/51/286-48). Para 3.1: the rules are the Income Tax (Deduction for Expenses in relation to Secretarial Fee and Tax Filing Fee) (Amendment) Rules 2021 [P.U.(A) 471/2021] amending the parent Rules 2020 [P.U.(A) 162/2020]. Para 4.1: effective YA 2022 the fee need only be INCURRED ('dilakukan') in the basis period - the previous requirement that it also be PAID is removed. Para 4.3/4.4: fees incurred in YA2020-YA2021 but not yet claimed are deductible only when paid in YA2022 or later; anything incurred BEFORE the basis period for YA2020 and still unclaimed cannot be claimed from 1 January 2022.
- SECRETARIAL & TAX FILING FEES - the cap is a SINGLE COMBINED RM15,000 per year of assessment covering both fee types (guideline table at para 4.2, and Example 2 where RM6,000 secretarial + RM5,000 + RM5,000 tax filing = RM16,000 incurred is expressly restricted: 'jumlah potongan yang dibenarkan untuk keseluruhan perbelanjaan bagi TT 2022 adalah terhad kepada RM15,000 sahaja walaupun perbelanjaan sebenar yang dilakukan berjumlah RM16,000'). The guideline's own history table (para 1.3) records that under the earlier P.U.(A) 336/2014 (YA2015-YA2019, revoked by P.U.(A) 162/2020) the two fees carried SEPARATE figures of RM5,000 and RM10,000. Effective-dated: combined RM15,000 applies from YA2020 (P.U.(A) 162/2020) and continues under the YA2022 incurred-basis amendment.
- SECRETARIAL FEE - what qualifies (guideline paras 5.1-5.2): fees paid to a company secretary REGISTERED UNDER THE COMPANIES ACT 2016, for secretarial services to comply with statutory requirements under the Companies Act 2016 - advisory on company meetings, preparation of directors' resolutions, share issuance, lodgement of prescribed forms under CA 2016 and other company matters.
- TAX FILING FEE - what qualifies is a CLOSED LIST (guideline para 6.1): (a) submission of the income tax return form (BNCP) under ITA ss.77, 77A, 77B, 83 and 86; (b) submission of tax estimate/instalment forms under ITA s.107C, i.e. CP204, CP204A and CP204B; (c) returns prescribed under s.26 Sales Tax Act 2018 [Act 806]; (d) returns prescribed under s.26 Service Tax Act 2018 [Act 807]; (e) returns under s.19 Departure Levy Act 2019 [Act 813]; (f) returns under s.19 Tourism Tax Act 2017 [Act 791]. Para 6.2: the fee must be SPECIFICALLY for the filing of those tax/levy forms, including the tax agent's advisory relating to the computation of the company's tax and departure levy.
- SECRETARIAL & TAX FILING FEES - what does NOT qualify (guideline para 7, verbatim in substance): incidental expenses such as reimbursement / out-of-pocket expenses, telephone and fax, printing, stationery, postage, travel and accommodation; and for secretarial fees, AGM (mesyuarat agung) expenses are additionally excluded. So a tax-agent or secretary invoice that bundles disbursements must be split at posting or the whole line is unauditable.
- ZAKAT - ITA s.44(11A) (verbatim): 'There shall be deducted pursuant to this subsection from the AGGREGATE INCOME of a person other than an offshore company excluding chargeable offshore company AND INDIVIDUAL for the relevant year reduced by any deduction for that year in accordance with subsection (1) an amount equal to the payment of ZAKAT PERNIAGAAN which is paid in the basis period for that relevant year to an appropriate religious authority established under any written law or any person authorized by such religious authority: Provided that the amount to be deducted pursuant to this subsection shall not exceed ONE-FORTIETH of the aggregate income of that person in the relevant year.' It is therefore (i) a company-level deduction, (ii) at the aggregate-income stage not the s.33 stage, (iii) capped at 1/40 (2.5%) of aggregate income, (iv) restricted to zakat PERNIAGAAN paid to an authorised religious authority, (v) on a PAID basis.
- ZAKAT - the s.6A rebate is NOT available to a Sdn Bhd. ITA s.6A(1) applies the rebate mechanism to 'the chargeable income of every INDIVIDUAL RESIDENT for the basis year for that year'; s.6A(3) grants the rebate 'for any zakat, fitrah or any other Islamic religious dues payment of which is obligatory and which are paid in the basis year ... to, and evidenced by a receipt issued by, an appropriate [religious authority]'. So company zakat = s.44(11A) deduction; individual zakat = s.6A(3) rebate. The two must never share a ledger line.
- DONATIONS - ITA s.44(6): deduction from aggregate income for 'any gift of MONEY made by him in the basis year for that year to the Government, a State Government, a local authority or an institution or organization or a fund, APPROVED for the purposes of this section by the Director General on the application of the institution or organization concerned: Provided that the amount to be deducted from the aggregate income for the relevant year in respect of any gift of money made to any INSTITUTION, ORGANIZATION OR FUND approved ... shall not exceed TEN PER CENT of the aggregate income of that person in the relevant year.' By its terms the 10% proviso attaches to the approved institution/organisation/fund limb, not to the Government / State Government / local authority limb. s.44(7) defines the qualifying institution and organisation categories exhaustively.
- DONATIONS - the neighbouring subsections are separate regimes with separate ceilings and separate valuation authorities, so they cannot be merged: s.44(6A) gift of artefact, manuscript or painting to the Government/State Government, value determined by the Department of Museums Malaysia or the National Archives; s.44(8) gift of money for public library facilities, 'not exceeding twenty thousand ringgit', and only for a person to whom s.34(6)(g) does not apply; s.44(9) and s.44(10) are restricted to 'a relevant person who is an INDIVIDUAL'; s.44(11) gift of painting to the National Art Gallery or a state art gallery, value determined by that gallery; s.44(11B) gift of money for a Minister-approved SPORTS activity; s.44(11C) gift of money or contribution in kind for a Minister-approved PROJECT OF NATIONAL INTEREST. s.44(11B) and (11C) each cap at 'the difference between the amount of ten per cent of the aggregate income ... and the total amount that has been deducted pursuant to the proviso to subsections (6), (11B)/(11C) and (11D)' - a SHARED 10% envelope that can only be computed if each stream is separately totalled.
- s.34(6) SPECIAL DEDUCTIONS (each its own line, each with its own condition or cap): (a) payroll tax paid; (b) turnover tax; (e) expenditure on the provision of equipment, or alteration/renovation of premises, necessary to assist any DISABLED person employed in the production of gross income; (f) expenditure on translation into or publication in the national language of cultural, literary, professional, scientific or technical books APPROVED BY DEWAN BAHASA DAN PUSTAKA; (g) provision of library facilities accessible to the public and contributions to public/school/higher-education libraries, with the proviso 'the amount that may be deducted shall not exceed ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND RINGGIT'; (h) provision of services, public amenities and contributions to a charity or community project pertaining to education, health, housing, conservation/preservation of environment, enhancement of income of the poor, infrastructure, ICT, or maintenance of a heritage building.
- BAD DEBTS - ITA s.34(2): the deduction is computed DEBT BY DEBT - '(a) if at the end of the relevant period THE DEBT is reasonably estimated in all the circumstances of the case to be WHOLLY irrecoverable, an amount equal to the amount of the debt; (b) if ... PARTLY irrecoverable, an amount equal to so much of the debt as is estimated to be irrecoverable', reduced by any deduction already made for THAT debt in a prior year. s.34(3) confines 'debt' to a debt arising from a matter in s.24(1) or interest under s.24(5) whose amount 'has been included in the relevant gross income or in the gross income ... for a prior year', or a s.24(5) loan granted in the course of the business. A pooled/collective impairment charge cannot satisfy the per-debt estimate or the previously-taxed test.
- INTEREST - ITA s.33(1)(a) allows interest on money borrowed and '(i) employed in that period in the production of gross income from that source; or (ii) laid out on assets used or held in that period for [that purpose]'. ITA s.33(2) restricts the deduction where the borrower has made a loan or an investment 'otherwise than for the purpose of producing that gross income' and it appears to the DG that it 'has been financed wholly or partly or directly or indirectly out of the borrowed money' - the restriction is computed on a CALENDAR-MONTH basis from the month-end amount of the loan outstanding and the cost of the investment held. ITA s.33(4)/(5): a sum payable for a basis period but not DUE to be paid in that period is deducted when it becomes due, and the person must notify the DG in writing within twelve months of the end of that basis period.
- DIRECTORS' LOANS - ITA s.140B(1): 'where in a basis period for a year of assessment, a company makes any loan or advances of any money FROM THE INTERNAL FUNDS of the company to a person who is a DIRECTOR of that company, the company shall be deemed to have a gross income consisting of interest from such loan or advances for that basis period.' s.140B(2): the deemed interest for each calendar month is 1/12 x A x B, where A is 'the total amount of loan or advances outstanding AT THE END OF THE CALENDAR MONTH' and B is the average lending rate of commercial banks published by the Central Bank at the end of that month (or another prescribed reference rate). s.140B(3): if interest actually charged exceeds the deemed sum the section ceases to apply; if it is less, the section applies and the interest actually charged is disregarded.
- RECORDS (statute) - ITA s.82(1)(a): every person carrying on a business 'shall keep and retain in safe custody SUFFICIENT RECORDS for a period of SEVEN YEARS from the end of the year to which any income from that business relates to enable that income ... or the adjusted loss ... to be READILY ASCERTAINED by the Director General'. s.82(5): if the DG is of the opinion that the accounts or records produced 'are insufficient or inadequate', he may require financial statements made in accordance with the Companies Act 2016. s.82(6): 'appropriate entries to be made in those records in respect of transactions WITHIN SIXTY DAYS of each transaction.' s.82(8): 'All records that relate to any business in Malaysia shall be kept and retained IN MALAYSIA.' s.82(9): 'records' include books of account recording receipts and payments or income and expenditure, and invoices, vouchers, receipts and other documents necessary to verify the entries.
- RECORDS (deduction forfeiture) - ITA s.39(1A): where a person is required under s.81 to furnish information within the time specified in a notice and 'that information concerns wholly or in part a DEDUCTION CLAIMED by that person ... NO DEDUCTION from the gross income from that source for that period shall be allowed in respect of such claim if the person fails to provide such information within the time specified'. An unsupportable ledger line is therefore a forfeited deduction, not merely a queried one.
- RECORDS (ruling, and the chart-of-accounts point) - LHDN Public Ruling No. 4/2000 'Keeping Sufficient Records (Companies & Co-operatives)' (issued 01/03/2000, made under ITA s.82). Para 3.3.1: a company 'must keep records and books of accounts including a cash book, a sales ledger, a purchases ledger and a GENERAL LEDGER ... Appropriate entries for each transaction should be recorded as soon as possible (in any case not later than 60 days after the transaction)'. Para 3.3.2: the books 'should be sufficient to explain the transactions and to enable a true and fair profit and loss account and a balance sheet to be prepared'. Para 3.3.3 (directly on point for a designed CoA): 'The system documentation including the accounting manual, CHART AND CODE OF ACCOUNTS should be maintained.' Para 3.3.4: records kept at the registered office or business premises in Malaysia. Para 3.4.1: if sufficient records are not kept, 'The chargeable income of the company ... will be determined according to the BEST JUDGEMENT of the Director General'.
- RECORDS (company law) - Companies Act 2016 s.245(1): the company, its directors and managers shall cause accounting and other records to be kept 'to sufficiently explain the transactions and financial position of the company and enable true and fair profit and loss accounts and balance sheets ... to be prepared' and kept 'in a manner as to enable the accounting and other records to be conveniently and properly AUDITED'. s.245(2): entries within SIXTY DAYS of completion of the transactions. s.245(3): retain for SEVEN YEARS. s.245(4): kept at the registered office or such other place as the directors think fit. s.245(9): fine up to RM500,000 or imprisonment up to three years or both.
- INVESTMENT HOLDING COMPANIES - ITA s.60F(2) defines 'permitted expenses' by an exhaustive five-bucket list of expenses 'which are NOT deductible under subsection 33(1)': '(a) directors' fees; (b) wages, salaries and allowances; (c) management fees; (d) secretarial, audit and accounting fees, telephone charges, printing and stationary costs and postage; and (e) rent and other expenses incidental to the maintenance of an office.' Note that the statute itself puts DIRECTORS' FEES in a bucket separate from wages/salaries/allowances, and puts SECRETARIAL fees in the same bucket as audit and accounting fees - a company that may ever be an IHC must be able to report exactly these five totals.
- COMPANIES ACT 2016 s.74: 'All shares issued before or upon the commencement of this Act shall have NO PAR OR NOMINAL VALUE.' The concept of authorised/nominal capital on which the P.U.(A) 475/2003 incorporation-expense test turns (RM2,500,000 authorised capital) has no direct CA 2016 equivalent for companies incorporated under the 2016 Act.

### Accounts implied

- Entertainment - 100% deductible, sub-analysed by proviso (staff entertainment; entertainment supplied for payment in an entertainment business; promotional gifts at OVERSEAS trade fairs/exhibitions; promotional samples; public cultural/sporting events; promotional gifts WITH conspicuous logo within Malaysia; entertainment related wholly to sales i.e. customers/dealers/distributors only; local yearly-event family leave passage). RULE: ITA s.39(1)(l) provisos (i)-(viii); PR 4/2015 para 5. The proviso must be evidenced per transaction, so the proviso is a posting dimension, not a note.
- Entertainment - 50% deductible (passes s.33(1) but falls outside every proviso): supplier entertainment, gifts WITHOUT a business logo, festive hampers, flowers for a customer's new outlet. RULE: ITA s.39(1)(l) main limb; PR 4/2015 Table 1 items 9, 11, 12, 13. Merging this with the 100% bucket makes the add-back uncomputable.
- Entertainment - wholly non-deductible (fails s.33(1) itself): the company's own AGM entertainment, wedding gifts to customers, entertainment to employees of RELATED companies, cash contributions to a customer's function, entertainment to a potential customer in a closed transaction. RULE: PR 4/2015 Table 1 items 1, 3, 4, 5, 6. This is a 100% add-back, not a 50% one.
- Employee leave passage - overseas / non-qualifying (fully disallowed) held apart from the proviso (viii) local yearly-event family passage. RULE: ITA s.39(1)(m) read with s.39(1)(l)(viii).
- Incorporation expenses - QUALIFYING (six statutory heads only: M&A/prospectus printing and circulation; company registration and statutory documents incl. fees and stamp duty; preliminary contracts and stamp duty; debenture/share certificate/letter of allotment printing and stamp duty; company seal; underwriting commission), carried as a separately identifiable balance-sheet item until the commencement year. RULE: P.U.(A) 475/2003 as amended by P.U.(A) 472/2005; PR 11/2013 paras 8.2-8.4.
- Incorporation expenses - NON-QUALIFYING (secretarial fee on incorporation, service tax on it, travel reimbursement, photocopying, sundries). RULE: PR 11/2013 Example 5 - within one incorporation invoice bundle only the M&A printing and registration cost were allowed; the rest was struck out.
- Pre-commencement operating expenses - general (permanently non-deductible), analysed by nature so the exceptions can be lifted out. RULE: PR 11/2013 para 6.1 (not wholly and exclusively incurred in the production of income) with the commencement test at para 5.2.
- Pre-commencement APPROVED TRAINING expenses (P.U.(A) 160/1996), separate from ordinary staff training. RULE: PR 11/2013 para 10.2 - single deduction only if basic-skills training, incurred within ONE YEAR before commencement, and s.33-allowable; para 10.3 forfeits it if a Government training grant is received or a double deduction is claimed under P.U.(A) 61/1992.
- Schedule 4B qualifying pre-operational business expenditure (Minister-approved OVERSEAS business venture), sub-split feasibility study / market research / overseas fares / accommodation-and-sustenance-at-RM400-per-day. RULE: ITA Schedule 4B paras 1-4 - it is deducted under s.44(1) against AGGREGATE INCOME with its own carry-forward, so it can never sit in a business-source expense account.
- Hire-purchase ASSET at full cash cost, in the fixed asset register, one row per asset. RULE: ITA Schedule 3 para 46 deems the hirer the owner even though legal title stays with the financier (PR 5/2014 para 10).
- HP creditor - PRINCIPAL outstanding (current / non-current), separate from the asset and from trade payables. RULE: Schedule 3 para 46 makes QE the capital portion of instalments PAID, so principal movement must be readable off the ledger.
- HP unexpired / deferred interest (interest-in-suspense) contra-liability, separate from principal. RULE: Schedule 3 para 46 + PR 5/2014 para 10.1 - only the CAPITAL portion is QE; a net-of-interest creditor balance destroys the split.
- HP INTEREST expense (period charge), separate from all other finance cost. RULE: PR 5/2014 para 10.1.1 - HP interest is a revenue deduction against gross income while deposit and capital repayments are QE.
- HP deposit / down payment, identified in the year paid. RULE: PR 5/2014 para 10.1 - the deposit is part of the capital portion and enters QE in the year of payment.
- HP capital repayments PAID in the basis period (a cash-basis memo, not the amortisation schedule). RULE: PR 5/2014 para 10.1.2 - CA is computed on capital instalments that HAVE BEEN MADE, not the contractual schedule; early settlement and missed instalments both change QE.
- Motor vehicles (non-commercial) as a distinct asset class, one row per vehicle carrying cost, purchase date and a new/used flag. RULE: ITA Schedule 3 para 2(2) - QE capped at RM50,000, or RM100,000 only if unused before purchase and total cost <= RM150,000.
- Motor vehicle lease/rental expense, per vehicle, separate from other operating lease rentals. RULE: ITA s.39(1)(k) - the RM50,000/RM100,000 cap is cumulative ACROSS years 'in respect of that motor vehicle', which requires a per-vehicle running total.
- Directors' FEES (office; approved under CA 2016 s.230) - separate from Directors' SALARY/remuneration under a contract of service. RULE: EPF Act 1991 s.2 ties 'wages' to remuneration due to an EMPLOYEE UNDER A CONTRACT OF SERVICE, so the EPF obligation differs; CA 2016 s.230(2)-(3) requires Board approval subject to the constitution, minuted, with shareholder notification within 14 days; CA 2016 s.230(5) makes an unapproved payment a DEBT DUE BY THE DIRECTOR.
- Directors' fees PAYABLE (accrued but unpaid), as its own liability with the approval date and payment date. RULE: the company deducts on the s.33(1) incurred basis (s.33(4)-(5) where not yet due) while ITA s.25(1) taxes the director on RECEIPT - the two fall in different years and the bridge has to be a ledger balance.
- Directors' other benefits and compensation for loss of employment (office) - separate again. RULE: CA 2016 s.230(1)-(2) puts 'any benefits payable to the directors including any compensation for loss of employment' under the same approval gate as fees.
- Amount due FROM directors (loans and advances), reportable at each CALENDAR MONTH END, separate from other related-party and staff balances. RULE: ITA s.140B(1)-(2) - deemed interest income computed monthly as 1/12 x month-end outstanding x the reference lending rate; s.140B(3) makes the interest actually charged relevant only by comparison.
- Amount due TO directors (director's account / advances from directors), separate from amounts due FROM directors - never netted. RULE: netting hides the s.140B(2) month-end 'A' figure.
- EPF employer contribution (approved scheme) as its own expense line, linkable to the remuneration base per employee. RULE: ITA s.34(4) caps the deduction at the LESSER of the contribution or 19% of the employee's remuneration determined under the scheme rules - the test is uncomputable from a merged 'staff costs' figure.
- Contributions to NON-approved pension/provident/savings funds - separate and permanently disallowed. RULE: ITA s.39(1)(d).
- Secretarial fee (paid to a company secretary registered under CA 2016, for CA 2016 statutory compliance). RULE: LHDN Guideline LHDN.AG.600-1/7/3 (10.11.2023) paras 5.1-5.2, under P.U.(A) 162/2020 as amended by P.U.(A) 471/2021.
- Tax filing fee, and only for the closed list: ITRF under ITA ss.77/77A/77B/83/86; CP204, CP204A, CP204B under s.107C; sales tax returns under s.26 Act 806; service tax returns under s.26 Act 807; departure levy returns under s.19 Act 813; tourism tax returns under s.19 Act 791. RULE: same guideline para 6.1(a)-(f), with para 6.2 requiring the fee be specifically for those filings including advisory on the tax computation.
- Other tax and professional advisory fees (tax audit or investigation representation, appeals, transfer-pricing documentation, structuring advice) held OUTSIDE the secretarial/tax-filing accounts. RULE: they are absent from the closed list at guideline para 6.1, so pooling them inflates the RM15,000 claim and makes it indefensible.
- Secretarial and tax-agent DISBURSEMENTS / out-of-pocket (reimbursements, telephone and fax, printing, stationery, postage, travel, accommodation; plus AGM expenses on the secretarial side) as a separate non-qualifying account. RULE: guideline para 7 excludes them expressly - a bundled invoice must be split at posting.
- A combined-cap memo across secretarial + tax filing fee (single RM15,000 ceiling per YA, effective YA2020 under P.U.(A) 162/2020 and continuing under the YA2022 incurred-basis amendment). RULE: guideline para 4.2 and Example 2, where RM16,000 incurred was cut to RM15,000. Note the two fees carried SEPARATE RM5,000/RM10,000 figures under the earlier P.U.(A) 336/2014 for YA2015-2019, so historical comparatives must stay split.
- Audit fee and accounting fee, separate from secretarial fee. RULE: they are not within the secretarial/tax-filing Rules at all, and ITA s.60F(2)(d) groups 'secretarial, audit and accounting fees' as a distinct IHC permitted-expense bucket - both directions need the split.
- Zakat perniagaan PAID, as its own account outside operating expenses (ideally below the operating result), with the religious-authority payee and receipt reference. RULE: ITA s.44(11A) - it is an AGGREGATE-INCOME deduction on a PAID basis, capped at one-fortieth (2.5%) of aggregate income, restricted to zakat perniagaan paid to an appropriate religious authority; and ITA s.6A(1) confines the rebate alternative to resident INDIVIDUALS, so it must never merge with an individual's zakat.
- Donations - APPROVED institution / organisation / fund (s.44(6)), separate from Donations - Government / State Government / local authority (s.44(6)). RULE: the s.44(6) proviso caps only the approved-body limb at ten per cent of aggregate income; merging the two either wastes relief or overstates it.
- Donations - gift in kind streams, one line each: s.44(6A) artefact/manuscript/painting to Government (valued by the Department of Museums Malaysia or National Archives); s.44(8) public library gift (RM20,000 ceiling, and only where s.34(6)(g) does not apply); s.44(11) painting to the National/state art gallery (valued by that gallery); s.44(11B) approved sports activity; s.44(11C) approved project of national interest. RULE: each has its own ceiling and its own valuation authority, and s.44(11B)/(11C) draw on a SHARED 10%-of-aggregate-income envelope with s.44(6) and (11D) that can only be computed if each stream is separately totalled.
- Donations / sponsorships / CSR that are NOT to an approved body - separate, permanently disallowed. RULE: s.44(6) requires DG approval of the recipient; anything else is neither a s.33(1) expense nor a s.44 deduction.
- s.34(6) special-deduction lines, one each: payroll tax; turnover tax; equipment or premises alteration/renovation to assist a DISABLED employee; DBP-approved translation/publication; public library provision (RM100,000 ceiling); community project / public amenities. RULE: ITA s.34(6)(a),(b),(e),(f),(g),(h) - each is a distinct statutory head, and (g) has its own cap and interacts with s.44(8).
- Bad debts written off - SPECIFIC, per debtor, traceable to an invoice previously taken into gross income; and Doubtful debts - SPECIFIC allowance, per debtor; both held apart from any general / collective / expected-credit-loss provision. RULE: ITA s.34(2)(a)-(b) requires a per-debt estimate at the end of the relevant period net of prior-year deductions, and s.34(3) restricts 'debt' to amounts already included in gross income - a pooled ECL charge satisfies neither test.
- Bad debts RECOVERED, separate from write-offs. RULE: s.34(2)'s reduction mechanism for deductions already taken means recoveries must be identifiable against the same debt.
- Interest expense analysed by the borrowing it arises on, with the non-trade loans and investments funded by borrowings carried at CALENDAR MONTH-END balances. RULE: ITA s.33(1)(a) allows interest only to the extent the borrowed money is employed in producing gross income or laid out on assets so used, and s.33(2) computes the restriction month by month from the month-end loan outstanding and investment cost.
- Accrued expenses where the sum is payable for the period but not yet DUE to be paid, separately flagged. RULE: ITA s.33(4)-(5) defers the deduction to the year the sum becomes due and requires written notification to the DG within twelve months of the end of that basis period.
- Fixed asset register at asset level within each Schedule 3 class, recording additions, disposals, and ownership plus use at the END of the basis period. RULE: ITA Schedule 3 para 13(a) denies the allowance if at the end of the basis period the person was not the owner or the asset was not in use for the business; disposals trigger balancing adjustments per asset.
- For any company that may be an investment holding company: the five ITA s.60F(2) permitted-expense buckets held separately - (a) directors' fees, (b) wages/salaries/allowances, (c) management fees, (d) secretarial, audit and accounting fees plus telephone, printing, stationery and postage, (e) rent and office maintenance. RULE: ITA s.60F(2) defines permitted expenses by exactly these buckets.
- A maintained accounting manual and chart-and-code-of-accounts document alongside the ledger itself. RULE: LHDN PR 4/2000 para 3.3.3 - 'The system documentation including the accounting manual, chart and code of accounts should be maintained'; backed by ITA s.82(1)(a) (sufficient records, readily ascertainable, seven years), s.82(6) (entries within 60 days), s.82(8) (kept in Malaysia), PR 4/2000 para 3.4.1 (best-judgement assessment if records are insufficient), and ITA s.39(1A) (deduction FORFEITED if s.81 information on a claimed deduction is not supplied in time).

### Uncertain — do not encode

- The RM2,500,000 'authorised capital' condition in P.U.(A) 475/2003 has no clean Companies Act 2016 equivalent. CA 2016 s.74 abolished par/nominal value (verified from the AGC text), so companies incorporated from 31 January 2017 have no authorised capital figure to test. I found NO LHDN pronouncement reconciling the Rules with the no-par-value regime. Treat the incorporation-expense claim for a post-2017 Sdn Bhd as an open position and keep the six statutory heads separately identifiable regardless.
- I did NOT read the gazette text of P.U.(A) 475/2003 or P.U.(A) 472/2005 (Incorporation Expenses Rules) directly - the AGC federal gazette portal was not reachable from this environment. Everything stated about them comes from LHDN PR 11/2013 paras 8.1-8.4, which is an official LHDN source but a secondary statement of the Rules. Same for P.U.(A) 162/2020 and P.U.(A) 471/2021 (secretarial/tax filing fee), where I relied on the LHDN guideline of 10 November 2023 rather than the gazette.
- KWSP's own published administrative list of 'payments liable / not liable for EPF contribution' could NOT be verified - kwsp.gov.my returned HTTP 403 to every attempt. What IS verified is the statutory basis: EPF Act 1991 s.2 defines 'wages' as remuneration due to an EMPLOYEE UNDER A CONTRACT OF SERVICE and 'employee' by reference to a contract of service. The common practitioner statement that 'directors' fees are not subject to EPF' follows from that definition but I have not confirmed it on a KWSP page.
- The EPF Act text I used is the AGC reprint 'incorporating all amendments up to 1 January 2006'. Later amendments (including the 2019 amendment Act) were not checked. The 'wages' and 'employee' definitions are long-standing, but confirm against a current reprint before hard-coding EPF logic.
- I could NOT verify what the LHDN Form C / tax computation itself prescribes as separately-disclosed line items - the hasil.gov.my forms and guidebook pages returned 404. Any claim that 'the return form requires line X separately' is unverified here; the granularity findings above rest on the statute, the Public Rulings and the guideline instead.
- PR 4/2000 para 3.3.5 states records are to be retained 'for at least SIX years'. The current ITA s.82(1)(a) says SEVEN years, as does Companies Act 2016 s.245(3). PR 4/2000 was issued 01/03/2000 and predates the amendment; there is an amended edition dated 30.06.2001 that I could only locate in Bahasa Malaysia (KU4_2000_Pin.pdf) and could not fetch in English. Use SEVEN years - the statute governs.
- PR 4/2015 (Entertainment) is dated 29 July 2015 and states at para 2.1 that it accounts for law in force as at that date. The LHDN public rulings index I fetched showed no superseding entertainment ruling, but I did not exhaustively verify that none has been issued since; also PR 5/2014 (hire purchase / ownership of asset, 27 June 2014) and PR 11/2013 (pre-operational, 18 November 2013) are similarly dated - re-check the index before treating them as current.
- Whether tax-agent fees for tax AUDIT or INVESTIGATION representation, APPEALS (Form Q), or TRANSFER PRICING documentation qualify under the secretarial/tax-filing Rules: they do not appear in the closed list at guideline para 6.1(a)-(f), and para 6.2 requires the fee be specifically for filing the listed forms including advisory on the tax computation. I read that as excluding them, but the guideline does not say so expressly. Post them separately and flag the position rather than assuming.
- The historical split of the cap under the revoked P.U.(A) 336/2014 (YA2015-YA2019) - my reading is RM5,000 for secretarial fee and RM10,000 for tax filing fee - comes from a table in the LHDN guideline whose column alignment did not survive PDF text extraction cleanly. The COMBINED RM15,000 for YA2020 onward is solid (proved by guideline Example 2). Verify the 5,000/10,000 split against P.U.(A) 336/2014 itself if comparatives matter.
- ITA s.140B(2) defines the reference rate as 'the average lending rate of commercial banks published by the Central Bank at the end of the calendar month or where there is no such average lending rate, such other reference lending rate as may be prescribed by the Director General'. Bank Negara's Average Lending Rate publication has changed over time; I did not verify which rate LHDN currently prescribes. Design the directors'-loan account to carry a rate field rather than a hard-coded rate.
- None of the sources says in terms 'you must open a separate GL account for X'. The closest direct statement is PR 4/2000 para 3.3.3 (maintain the accounting manual, chart and code of accounts) plus ITA s.82(1)(a) ('readily ascertained') and s.39(1A) (deduction forfeited if the supporting information is not produced). The account-level splits in accounts_implied are the engineering consequence of per-transaction statutory tests, not quotations from a rule that names accounts.
- SST / MyInvois-driven account granularity, withholding-tax-driven splits under s.39(1)(f)/(i)/(j)/(q)/(s), s.140C interest limitation, and the reinvestment-allowance / incentive regimes were outside the scope of this brief and were not researched. Each is likely to add further mandatory splits to a Malaysian SME chart of accounts.

## MPERS / Companies Act 2016 — presentation and disclosure

### Verified facts

- MPERS LIFECYCLE: MASB issued MPERS 14 Feb 2014, effective for annual periods beginning on/after 1 Jan 2016. MPERS (2016) 'shall hereby be withdrawn for application for financial statements with annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027'. MPERS (2025) was issued 10 Oct 2025, 'aligns with the third edition of IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard issued by the IASB in February 2025', effective annual periods beginning on/after 1 Jan 2027, earlier application permitted. [masb.org.my/pages.php?id=20]
- MPERS PROVENANCE: 'MPERS is based substantially on the International Financial Reporting Standard for Small and Medium-sized Entities (IFRS for SMEs) issued by the IASB in July 2009... MPERS is a self-contained Standard that comes with 35 sections'. Amendments to MPERS (Oct 2015) are 'equivalent to the Amendments to IFRS for SMEs issued by the IASB in May 2015'. [MASB, Tan Liong Tong, 'A Comparative Analysis of PERS, MPERS and MFRS Frameworks', p.1]
- Q1 CONFIRMED — MPERS SECTION 20 IS *NOT* IFRS 16. MASB's own comparative analysis states MPERS Section 20 'Uses a "risks and rewards" approach to the classification of leases. A lease is classified as a finance lease if it transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership. All other leases that do not meet this transfer of risks and rewards criterion are classified as operating leases [S20.4]'. Indicators are 'similar to those in MFRS 117. However, no thresholds or bright lines are specified [S20.5 & S20.6]'. 'For a finance lease, a lessee shall capitalise the leased asset and the corresponding lease liability [S20.9]. The subsequent measurement of the lease liability is at amortised cost effective interest.' The MPERS/MFRS comparison column reads 'As described in MPERS' against MFRS 117 (i.e. the pre-IFRS-16 model). [MASB comparative analysis, section E Leases]
- Q1 CONFIRMED — MPERS (2025) DOES *NOT* CHANGE THE LESSEE MODEL. In the IASB's third-edition (Feb 2025) marked-up standard — the edition MPERS (2025) aligns to — para 20.4 still reads verbatim: 'A lease is classified as a finance lease if it transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership. A lease is classified as an operating lease if it does not transfer substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership.' Paras 20.5-20.8 (classification examples/indicators), the finance-lease lessee recognition/measurement text, and 20.13 disclosures carry no substantive markup change. The finance-vs-operating distinction survives into MPERS (2025). [ifrs.org 2025 markup PDF, pp.115-116]
- Q1 — LESSEE RECOGNITION (MPERS 20.9/20.10): 'At the commencement of the lease term, a lessee shall recognise its rights of use and obligations under finance leases as ASSETS AND LIABILITIES in its statement of financial position at amounts equal to the fair value of the leased property or, if lower, the present value of the minimum lease payments, determined at the inception of the lease. Any initial direct costs of the lessee (incremental costs that are directly attributable to negotiating and arranging a lease) are added to the amount recognised as an asset.' Discount rate = 'the interest rate implicit in the lease. If this cannot be determined, the lessee's incremental borrowing rate shall be used.' [Module 20 / 2025 markup, 20.9-20.10]

A lessee shall charge CONTINGENT RENTS as expenses in the periods in which they are incurred.' [Module 20, 20.11]
- Q1 — DEPRECIATION (MPERS 20.12): 'A lessee shall depreciate an asset leased under a finance lease in accordance with the relevant section of this Standard for that type of asset, for example, Section 17 Property, Plant and Equipment... ### Accounts implied

- Motor vehicles held under hire purchase / finance lease — COST (separate PPE class from owned motor vehicles). Rule: MPERS 20.13(a) requires the net carrying amount 'for each class of asset' held under finance lease, and 4.11(a) requires PPE 'in classifications appropriate to the entity'. You cannot report 20.13(a) if HP vehicles are commingled with owned ones.
- Accumulated depreciation — motor vehicles under HP/finance lease (separate contra). Rule: MPERS 20.13(a) needs the NET carrying amount by class; and 20.12 may impose a DIFFERENT useful life ('shorter of the lease term and its useful life') when there is no reasonable certainty ownership passes — so the depreciation run is not the same as for owned vehicles.
- Hire purchase / finance lease liability — CURRENT portion. Rule: MPERS 4.4 + 4.7(c) (due to be settled within twelve months). Note this obligation comes from Section 4, NOT Section 20 — Section 20 never mentions a current/non-current split.
- Hire purchase / finance lease liability — NON-CURRENT portion. Rule: MPERS 4.4 + 4.8 ('all other liabilities as non-current').
- Finance charge / HP interest expense — its own P&L account, mapped to the 'Finance costs' face line. Rule: MPERS 20.11 requires apportionment of minimum lease payments between the finance charge and the reduction of the liability using the effective interest method; MPERS 5.5(b) makes 'finance costs' a MINIMUM face line. HP interest must never be buried in the asset cost or in a generic 'bank charges'.
- Unexpired / deferred HP finance charges (interest-in-suspense contra to the HP liability) — needed by any firm that books the HP contract at gross instalment value. Rule: MPERS 20.9 measures the liability at fair value or lower PV of minimum lease payments, and 20.11 releases interest on an effective-interest basis; a gross-booking chart needs the contra to reconcile to the 20.9/20.11 amortised-cost carrying amount.
- Contingent rent expense — separate from base lease/HP charges. Rule: MPERS 20.11 final sentence: 'A lessee shall charge contingent rents as expenses in the periods in which they are incurred' (not spread), and 20.13(c) requires a description of contingent rent arrangements.
- A finance-lease maturity schedule capturing UNDISCOUNTED future minimum lease payments in three bands: ≤1 year / >1 and ≤5 years / >5 years. Rule: MPERS 20.13(b). This is a gross, undiscounted figure that CANNOT be derived from the discounted balance-sheet liability — it needs its own sub-ledger or memo schedule per contract.
- Operating lease rental expense — separate from finance-lease depreciation and finance charge. Rule: MPERS retains the finance/operating distinction (20.4), so operating leases stay off-balance-sheet with a straight-line expense (20.15) and their own commitment disclosure (20.16). A chart built for IFRS 16 (single right-of-use model) is WRONG for a Malaysian private entity.
- Investment property at COST less accumulated depreciation and impairment — separate from Investment property at FAIR VALUE THROUGH PROFIT OR LOSS. Rule: MPERS 4.2(ea) and 4.2(f) are two distinct minimum face lines. Most basic charts carry a single 'Investment property'.
- Biological assets at cost less depreciation/impairment — separate from biological assets at FVTPL. Rule: MPERS 4.2(h) and 4.2(i) — two distinct minimum face lines (relevant to plantation/agri SMEs, and MPERS Section 34).
- Current tax asset and current tax liability, kept separate from Deferred tax asset and Deferred tax liability — and the deferred tax accounts flagged ALWAYS NON-CURRENT. Rule: MPERS 4.2(n) and 4.2(o); 4.2(o) carries the explicit parenthetical 'these shall always be classified as non-current'. A chart that ages deferred tax by maturity will misclassify.
- Investments in associates — separate from Investments in jointly controlled entities — separate from other financial assets. Rule: MPERS 4.2(j), 4.2(k), and 4.2(c) which explicitly excludes (j) and (k) from 'financial assets'.
- Trade receivables — AMOUNTS DUE FROM RELATED PARTIES (as its own account, and further split by the four MPERS 33.10 categories). Rule: MPERS 4.11(b) requires trade and other receivables 'showing separately amounts due from related parties'; MPERS 33.10 requires the 33.9 disclosures separately for (a) entities with control/joint control/significant influence over the entity, (b) entities the entity controls or influences, (c) KMP of the entity or its parent in aggregate, (d) other related parties.
- Accrued income not yet billed (unbilled receivables) — a distinct receivable account, not lumped into trade debtors or prepayments. Rule: MPERS 4.11(b) names it as its own required sub-classification.
- Inventories split three ways: finished goods held for sale / work in progress ('in the process of production for such sale') / raw materials and consumables ('materials or supplies to be consumed in the production process or in the rendering of services'). Rule: MPERS 4.11(c)(i)-(iii).
- Trade payables split four ways: amounts payable to TRADE SUPPLIERS / payable to RELATED PARTIES / DEFERRED INCOME / ACCRUALS. Rule: MPERS 4.11(d). A chart with only 'Accounts Payable' and 'Accruals' fails two of the four; deferred income sitting inside 'other payables' is a common miss.
- Provisions for EMPLOYEE BENEFITS as an account distinct from OTHER provisions (warranty, restructuring, dismantling) — and each split current/non-current. Rule: MPERS 4.2(p) makes 'provisions' a minimum face line; 4.11(e) requires the employee-benefit vs other split; 4.4-4.8 force the current/non-current allocation. Provisions must also not sit inside trade and other payables (4.2(m) excludes (p) from financial liabilities).
- Non-controlling interest within equity, separate from equity attributable to owners of the parent. Rule: MPERS 4.2(q) and 4.2(r) (consolidated statements only).
- A separate equity account for EACH reserve, each with a description, and each OCI item accumulated in its own reserve. Rule: MPERS 4.11(f) (classes of equity, including OCI items 'presented separately in equity') and 4.12(b) ('a description of each reserve within equity').
- Share capital by CLASS (ordinary, each class of preference), with a memo of shares authorised / issued and fully paid / issued but not fully paid, plus a movement reconciliation of share numbers. Rule: MPERS 4.12(a)(i),(ii),(iv). MALAYSIAN OVERRIDE: do NOT create a Share Premium account and do not carry par value — CA 2016 s.74 (no par or nominal value) and s.618(2) (share premium and capital redemption reserve became part of share capital) make MPERS 4.11(f)'s 'share premium' and 4.12(a)(iii)'s 'par value' inoperative in Malaysia.
- Treasury shares / shares in the entity held by the entity, its subsidiaries or associates. Rule: MPERS 4.12(a)(vi).
- Shares reserved for issue under options and contracts for the sale of shares (memo, with terms and amounts). Rule: MPERS 4.12(a)(vii).
- Finance costs as a discrete P&L caption, distinct from operating expenses. Rule: MPERS 5.5(b) — one of only nine minimum income-statement face lines.
- Share of profit or loss of associates and of jointly controlled entities (equity method) — a P&L face line. Rule: MPERS 5.5(c). Separately, share of OCI of associates/JCEs. Rule: MPERS 5.5(h).
- Tax expense as a single face line EXCLUDING tax allocated to discontinued operations and to OCI items. Rule: MPERS 5.5(d) read with 29.35 — so the tax charge must be tagged by the item it relates to, which a single 'Taxation' account cannot do.
- Discontinued operations — a single post-tax amount combining the operation's post-tax result AND the post-tax gain/loss on impairment/reversal/disposal of its assets. Rule: MPERS 5.5(e)(i)-(ii). Requires the chart to segregate a discontinued component's results and disposal effects.
- Each OCI item by NATURE, tagged into two groups: those that will NOT be reclassified to profit or loss, and those that WILL be reclassified when conditions are met. Rule: MPERS 5.5(g)(i)-(ii). This is a required attribute on every OCI account, not just a grouping in the report.
- An expense FUNCTION tag (cost of sales / distribution costs / administrative expenses / other) on every expense account. Rule: not from MPERS 5.5 (which does not mandate function) but from SSM MBRS — the FS-MPERS template set is built on 'Statement of Profit or Loss - by Function of Expenses' plus a mandatory 'Analysis of Profit or Loss - by Function of Expense' tab, so the filing cannot be produced from a purely by-nature chart.
- Directors' fees — its own account. Rule: CA 2016 s.249(4)(a) (directors' remuneration in the notes, notwithstanding the accounting standards) and Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(a), which requires 'fees and other benefits DISTINGUISHED SEPARATELY'.
- Directors' salaries, bonuses and commissions — separate from directors' fees and separate from staff payroll. Rule: Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(a) enumerates 'fees, percentages, bonuses, commissions' as components to be distinguished.
- Directors' retirement / pension benefits (directors' EPF and any pension or retirement benefit scheme contributions) — separate from staff EPF. Rule: CA 2016 s.249(4)(b) and Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(a) ('any contribution in respect of them under any pension or retirement benefit scheme').
- Compensation to directors for loss of office — its own account. Rule: CA 2016 s.249(4)(c) and Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(a). It is a named statutory line even when the amount is nil in most years.
- Directors' benefits-in-kind — the estimated money value of benefits received otherwise than in cash (company car, accommodation, driver). Rule: Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(b) ('by way of a note or otherwise, the estimated money value of any other benefits received or receivable by them otherwise than in cash'). This is a memo/valuation account with no cash counterpart, which basic charts never carry.
- Amount due from directors — loans, quasi-loans and other dealings in favour of directors — a balance-sheet account distinct from ordinary related-party receivables. Rule: CA 2016 s.249(4)(d). Note the statutory term 'quasi-loans and other dealings' is wider than a formal loan, so the account must catch informal current-account drawings.
- Fees paid to a third party for services provided to the company by a director or past director. Rule: Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(c) — 'the total of the amount paid to or receivable by any third party in respect of the services provided... by any director or past director'. A payment to an intermediary, not to the director, so it never lands in a directors' remuneration account by default.
- Professional fees paid to a director, or to a firm of which a director is a member, acting in a PROFESSIONAL capacity. Rule: the proviso to Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(a) — such amounts are excluded from directors' remuneration BUT 'shall be shown separately whether by way of note or otherwise'. So it needs its own account precisely because it is NOT remuneration.
- Directors'/officers'/auditor's indemnity and insurance (D&O premium). Rule: Fifth Schedule Part I para 2(d) — 'the total amount, if any, of any indemnity given to or insurance effected for, any director, officer or auditor of the company'. Usually buried inside a general 'Insurance' account.
- Auditors' remuneration — statutory audit fee, split from non-audit/other services, and capturing amounts 'given by or from the company OR BY OR FROM ANY SUBSIDIARY'. Rule: CA 2016 s.249(4)(e), which defines the disclosure as remuneration 'for their services AS AUDITORS, inclusive of all fees, percentages or other payments or consideration' — the 'as auditors' qualifier is what forces the audit vs non-audit split, and the subsidiary clause forces group-level capture.
- Key management personnel compensation (aggregate) — a tag spanning directors AND non-director senior management, covering all Section 28 employee benefits including share-based payment and amounts paid ON BEHALF of the entity by a parent or shareholder. Rule: MPERS 33.6 + 33.7. This set is WIDER than the CA 2016 'directors' set, so the chart needs a KMP dimension as well as a directors' dimension.
- Amounts due from / due to related parties, split into the four MPERS 33.10 categories: (a) holding company and other entities with control/joint control/significant influence over the entity; (b) subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures; (c) key management personnel of the entity or its parent (in aggregate); (d) other related parties (director-related companies, close family members). Rule: MPERS 33.10 requires the 33.9 disclosures 'separately for each of the following categories'. One catch-all related-party account is non-compliant.
- Related-party balances flagged SECURED vs UNSECURED, with terms, settlement consideration, and guarantees given/received. Rule: MPERS 33.9(b)(i)-(ii) — 'their terms and conditions, including whether they are secured and the nature of the consideration to be provided in settlement' and 'details of any guarantees given or received'.
- Provision for uncollectable receivables from RELATED PARTIES, and the bad/doubtful debt EXPENSE in respect of related parties — both separate from the general doubtful-debt provision and general bad-debt expense. Rule: MPERS 33.9(c) and 33.9(d) name these as two distinct minimum disclosures.
- Bad debts WRITTEN OFF as an account distinct from PROVISION/allowance for doubtful debts. Rule: CA 2016 Fifth Schedule Part I para 1(g)-(h) requires the directors to attest separately that 'all known bad debts had been written off' and that 'adequate provision had been made for doubtful debts' — two amounts, two attestations.
- Dividends DECLARED / recommended, tracked separately from dividends PAID, and dated relative to the previous financial year end. Rule: CA 2016 Fifth Schedule Part I para 1(f) — the amount recommended by directors, plus amounts paid or declared since the end of the previous financial year, indicating which were already shown in a previous report.
- Transfers to and from reserves and provisions, with particulars. Rule: CA 2016 Fifth Schedule Part I para 1(b) requires 'the amounts and particulars of any material transfers to or from reserves or provisions' — so reserve movements need their own transaction trail, not just an opening/closing balance.
- Related-party transaction TYPE tagging on revenue, purchases, asset disposals, services, leases, licence/royalty flows, loans and equity contributions, guarantees, and settlements of liabilities on behalf of another party. Rule: MPERS 33.12(a)-(j) enumerates exactly these as transactions that must be disclosed when with a related party — and 33.12(i) ('settlement of liabilities on behalf of the entity or by the entity on behalf of another party') is the one a basic chart routes straight to a director's current account with no transaction identity.
- Ultimate holding company identity, and the parent-subsidiary relationship recorded even in a year with NO transactions. Rule: MPERS 33.5 ('Relationships between a parent and its subsidiaries shall be disclosed irrespective of whether there have been transactions between them') and CA 2016 Fifth Schedule Part I para 4 (name the ultimate holding company).
- A retained earnings movement structure capable of producing a standalone Statement of Retained Earnings. Rule: SSM added a 'Statement of Retained Earnings' to the MPERS FS and KFI taxonomy in SSMxT 2022 (it does not exist in the MFRS FS set), reflecting MPERS Section 6's combined statement of income and retained earnings.
- Sub-classification children beneath EVERY statement-of-financial-position face line, granular enough to avoid mapping to 'Others'. Rule: the MBRS FS-MPERS 'Sub-classification of Assets, Liabilities and Equity' tab holds 'the breakdowns for the corresponding element labels in the SOFP' for all face lines, and SSM's guide requires a mandatory explanatory FOOTNOTE for any many-to-one mapping or any amount mapped to 'Others'.

- MPERS (2025) Section 20 paragraph NUMBERING may shift slightly. The third-edition markup shows the headings 'Initial recognition' and 'Subsequent measurement' being inserted as numbered paragraphs 20.9/20.11 with the substantive text pushed to 20.10/20.12. Content is unchanged, but if you cite a 2025-edition paragraph number for the recognition or effective-interest requirement, verify it against the clean (non-markup) MPERS (2025) PDF rather than the markup.
- I did not verify whether MASB made any Malaysian modification to Section 20, Section 4, Section 5 or Section 33 when issuing MPERS (2025). The MASB notice says only that MPERS (2025) 'aligns with the third edition'. Any MPERS-specific carve-out in those sections would not have shown up in my sources.
- The IASB's reasoning for NOT aligning Section 20 to IFRS 16 in the third edition (cost/benefit for SMEs, mixed respondent views) came from a search snippet referencing IASB staff paper AP30D 'Section 20 Leases and IFRS 16 Leases' (Jan 2024) — I did not fetch that paper. The OUTCOME (finance/operating distinction retained in the Feb 2025 third edition) IS verified directly from the third-edition text.
- The SSM Preparers' Guide is largely screenshot-based, so the extractable text confirms the STRUCTURE of the FS-MPERS templates (which statements and notes exist, and that related-party categories and 'all the mandatory items' are enforced) but NOT the individual element labels inside each template. I therefore could not confirm from a fetched page whether the FS-MPERS taxonomy contains explicit named tags such as 'Directors' remuneration - fees', 'benefits-in-kind', 'Amount due from directors', or 'hire purchase creditors'. The strong inference that it does rests on the 979 SSM extension elements being described as CA-2016-driven, plus s.249(4) being mandatory note content. To settle this, open the FS-MPERS template in mTool (or the SSMxT element list) and read the note tabs directly.
- Whether SSM/mTool VALIDATION actually blocks a submission that omits, say, the directors' benefits-in-kind tag is unverified — 'mandatory items' appears in the guide for the related-party note, but I could not confirm the full mandatory-field matrix for FS-MPERS.
- MBRS 2.0 mandatory-implementation timing (SSM announcement 26 Nov 2024, phased from 1 Dec 2024, previously exempt BNM-regulated institutions brought in) comes only from third-party search snippets (Crowe, BDO, InCorp, xbrl.org), not from a fetched SSM page. The SSM sources I did fetch are the mTool user manual (v2.4, Sept 2024), the Preparers' Guide (v1.0, Sept 2018) and the 2022 taxonomy consultative document — so the element counts I quote are SSMxT 2022 and may have moved in the MBRS 2.0 / current taxonomy.
- The claim that SSMxT was originally based on the IFRS Taxonomy 2017 with local customisation for CA 2016, MFRS and MPERS came from a search snippet of the SSM Taxonomy Architecture 2017 document, which I did not fetch. The figures I DID verify (564 IFRS-for-SMEs-2022 + 979 SSM = 1,543 for FS-MPERS) are from the fetched 2022 consultative document.
- MPERS 20.15/20.16 (operating-lease expense recognition and lessee operating-lease disclosures) are referenced in my answer from the module's paragraph index rather than quoted in full — I confirmed the paragraphs exist and their subject matter but did not extract their complete text.
- Scope question not investigated: whether the SME in question actually qualifies as a 'private entity' eligible for MPERS. MASB's Implementation page defines these as private companies under CA 2016 that are not required to file with the Securities Commission or Bank Negara. A company that files with either must use MFRS, and the whole Section 20 answer flips to MFRS 16 (single right-of-use model, no finance/operating distinction) — a materially different chart of accounts.
- Tax-driven accounts are outside what I researched. Malaysian HP is commonly subject to separate capital-allowance treatment (and Public Ruling guidance on HP assets), and the deferred-tax consequence of the MPERS book treatment will need its own accounts. Nothing here is verified against LHDN sources.
- The Ninth Schedule of the Companies Act 1965 (the old prescriptive list of accounts content) appeared in search results and is sometimes still cited in Malaysian practice material. It is repealed — CA 2016 replaced it with the s.249(4) 'as the Registrar may determine' mechanism plus the Fifth Schedule for the directors' report. I did not trace the repeal provision, but note that SSMxT retains separate 'CA 1965' templates for legacy filings (per the 2022 consultative document), so old-Act line items can still surface in filings for old financial years.
